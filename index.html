Institutional Non-Sponsorship Disclosure:
This survey and research project is an independent academic investigation conducted by Bruce Johnson. This study is NOT sponsored, funded, or mandated by Western Governors University (WGU).

1. Purpose of the Study: You are invited to take part in an educational research study examining whether starting with hands-on query mechanics before reading abstract theoretical concepts (such as normal forms and cardinality) influences study velocity and objective assessment outcomes in D426.

2. Age & Eligibility Requirement: You must be at least 18 years of age to participate in this study. If you are under 18 years of age, you are not eligible to take part.

3. Completely Voluntary Participation & No Grade Impact: Your participation is 100% voluntary. You may choose not to participate, or discontinue at any time. Opting out will have no impact on your course grade, academic standing, tuition, progress pacing, or access to course materials and faculty coaching.

4. Study Procedures & Time Commitment: If you consent to participate, you will complete a brief diagnostic survey (~3 minutes). Depending on study arm assignment, you may also receive optional access to an interactive browser-based SQL sandbox (~15 to 20 minutes; total time commitment approximately 20 to 25 minutes).

5. Privacy & Data Handling: Telemetry interactions and survey responses are securely linked with your student ID for academic matching. All data will be de-identified prior to statistical analysis. Only aggregate findings will ever be reported.

6. Contact Information: If you have questions about the research, contact Bruce Johnson at b.johnson1@wgu.edu. For questions regarding your rights as a research subject, you may contact the WGU Institutional Review Board at irb@wgu.edu.

Sandbox Scope & Exploratory Capabilities

This practice environment is an in-browser WebAssembly sandbox calibrated specifically for foundational DDL, DML, and DQL operations covered in D426 (table creation, basic constraints, batch inserts, controlled updates, filtering, and standard inner joins). It does not emulate production server features such as stored procedures, triggers, or custom user variables. For unrestricted database experimentation, we recommend using DB Fiddle.
