Notice Regarding WGU Sponsorship: This study is an independent academic research project conducted by Bruce Johnson, Instructor, Faculty Lead, School of Technology (b.johnson1@wgu.edu). It is NOT sponsored, funded, or required by Western Governors University (WGU).
1. What is this study about?
We are studying whether starting with hands-on SQL coding practice before reading database theory affects the time students spend on D426 and how they do on their final exam.
2. Age Requirement:
You must be at least 18 years old to take part in this study.
3. Your Choice (Voluntary):
Taking part is 100% voluntary. Choosing not to participate will have no effect on your course grade, academic progress, tuition, or your access to instructors, cohorts, and study resources. You may stop at any time.
4. What will you do?
If you choose to join, you will complete a quick 3-minute study preference check-in (~3 minutes). Depending on random group assignment, you may also get access to a 5-module interactive SQL practice sandbox (~15–20 minutes, ~20–25 minutes total). The optional SQL practice activities are designed for standard laptop and desktop web browsers.
5. Privacy & Your Information:
Your invitation link uses a pre-masked random study token instead of your WGU student ID, so your name, email, and institutional student ID never enter this website. Completion timestamps and survey responses are linked offline only to match course milestones, and all results are reported strictly in group summaries.
6. Questions & Contacts:
If you have questions about the study, contact Bruce Johnson at b.johnson1@wgu.edu. If you have questions about your rights as a participant, contact the WGU Institutional Review Board at irb@wgu.edu.

About This Practice Sandbox
This interactive practice environment is designed specifically for the foundational SQL commands in D426 (creating tables, adding rows, updating records, filtering queries, and joining tables). Advanced server features such as stored procedures and triggers are not needed here. If you ever want to experiment with larger multi-table databases on your own, you can also explore DB Fiddle.
