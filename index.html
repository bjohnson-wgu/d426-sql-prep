Hey there, and welcome to the D426 SQL Sandbox! First off, thank you so much for jumping in and participating in this research study—I’m genuinely excited you’re here. So, what is this all about? Well, when you’re starting out in Data Management Foundations, diving straight into dense textbook theory can feel a little overwhelming. We think that spending just about 30 minutes getting your hands dirty with a few of the most common, high-impact SQL commands right up front will help you hit the ground running—and potentially boost your Objective Assessment score and help you pass the course faster, too. Before we look at the screen, one quick tip: if you happen to be watching this on your phone or a tablet, you’ll have a much easier time completing these exercises on a laptop or desktop computer with a physical keyboard. Now, let’s take a quick tour of how this sandbox works. Across the top of the screen, you’ll see five modules that walk through the core building blocks of SQL: creating tables, adding data, updating data, querying data, and joining tables together. Each module follows the same simple rhythm: on the left side, you’ll see a short video from me explaining the big idea in plain English. On the right side is your live SQL editor—and here’s the best part: the starter code is already written for you! You have two ways you can use this, and both are great: first, if you want to move quickly, you can simply read through the code to see how it’s structured, click 'Run MySQL Code,' inspect the results table that pops up underneath, and click 'Proceed to Next Module.' You can cruise through all five modules in around 25 or 30 minutes. Second—and I really encourage this—you can treat this like a true sandbox and play around! Try changing values, adding your own rows, or even making a mistake on purpose—like deleting a semicolon or leaving off a WHERE clause. When an error happens, a troubleshooting drawer will pop up on the left with extra tips—and even bonus videos from me—walking through the most common mistakes students run into. And don't worry about breaking anything: you can hit 'Reset Template' anytime to snap right back to the original code. Your progress saves automatically in this browser, and you can use the link buttons at the top to copy or email yourself your personal link so you can step away and come back anytime. You can jump forward or backward between modules anytime using the tabs at the top or the 'Previous' and 'Next' buttons. When you finish Module 5, you’ll earn your completion badge, get a little confetti celebration, and unlock a next-steps guide for tackling the rest of D426. Ready to dive in? Click 'Let’s Get Started' below and I’ll see you in Module 1!

This survey and research project is an independent academic investigation conducted by Bruce Johnson. This study is NOT sponsored, funded, or mandated by Western Governors University (WGU).

1. Purpose of the Study: You are invited to take part in an educational research study examining whether starting with SQL coding practice before reading database theory influences time spent on this course and final exam outcomes in D426.

2. Age & Eligibility Requirement: You must be at least 18 years old to participate in this study. If you are under 18 years old, you are not eligible to take part.

3. Completely Voluntary Participation & No Grade Impact: Your participation is 100% voluntary. You may choose not to participate, or discontinue at any time. Opting out will have no impact on your course grade, academic standing, tuition, progress pacing, or access to course materials and faculty coaching.

4. Study Procedures & Time Commitment: If you consent to participate, you will complete a brief diagnostic survey (~3 minutes). Depending on study arm assignment, you may also receive optional access to an interactive browser-based SQL sandbox (~25 to 30 minutes).

5. Privacy & Data Handling: Telemetry interactions and survey responses are securely linked with your student ID for academic matching. All data will be de-identified prior to statistical analysis. Only aggregate findings will ever be reported.

6. Contact Information: If you have questions about the research, contact Bruce Johnson at b.johnson1@wgu.edu. For questions regarding your rights as a research subject, you may contact the WGU Institutional Review Board at irb@wgu.edu.

About This Practice Sandbox

This practice tool runs right inside your web browser and is built specifically for the core SQL commands you learn in D426 (creating tables, adding data, updating rows, searching and sorting data, and joining tables). It doesn't include advanced database server tools like triggers or stored procedures. Want to build bigger, unrestricted database projects on your own? Try DB Fiddle.
